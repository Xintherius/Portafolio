# Global Video Game Sales & Market Trends Analysis
**Author:** Andres Chisaca 

**Tools Used:** Tableau, SQL (MySQL), Data Processing, Exploratory Data Analysis

## Project Overview
This project is an exploratory data analysis and business intelligence study examining the historical commercial performance of over 16,000 video game transactions. The core objectives involved cleaning and modeling raw transaction records using SQL to design an interactive Tableau dashboard. The primary goal is to evaluate how regional consumer behavior, publisher market share concentration, and console lifecycles impact global software revenue.

## Key Insights
*   **Regional Market Divergence:** Consumer preferences vary significantly by geography. The Japanese market demonstrated consistent demand for Role-Playing Games (RPGs), whereas North American and European markets showed dominant preferences for Action, Shooter, and Sports titles.
*   **Publisher Concentration:** Major publishers such as Nintendo, Electronic Arts, and Activision maintained sustained revenue concentration across multi-generational console releases.
*   **Platform Lifecycle Dynamics:** Peak software sales volume consistently aligned with the 3- to 5-year post-launch maturity window for major console platforms.

## Strategic Recommendations
*   The data shows a stark contrast in regional preferences. The North American (NA) and European (PAL) markets are highly aligned in their massive consumption of Action, Sports, and Shooter games. Conversely, the Japanese (JP) market is dominated by Role-Playing Games. Marketing budgets and localization efforts should be heavily concentrated on these specific genres within their respective regions rather than applying a global, one-size-fits-all approach.
*   The PS2, Xbox 360, and PS3 represent the largest historical sales volumes by a significant margin. For studios considering remasters, remakes, or IP revivals, prioritizing franchises that originated or peaked on these specific platforms offers the highest probability of tapping into established, nostalgic consumer bases.
*   The Score Distribution chart indicates that the overwhelming majority of tracked titles achieve a "High (>7)" score. This establishes a baseline quality threshold; releasing a game that tests below a 7.0 score carries severe commercial risk, as the market is highly saturated with well-reviewed competitors.
